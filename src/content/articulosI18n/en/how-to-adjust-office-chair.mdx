A good office chair can still feel bad if it is set up wrong. Most people adjust the height once, ignore everything else and then blame the chair. The better approach is to tune the chair in a fixed order: feet, hips, lumbar support, arms and screen.

## Step 1: set seat height

Sit all the way back, relax your shoulders and place both feet flat on the floor. Your knees should be close to a right angle, with your hips at the same height or slightly higher. If your feet do not reach the floor, use a footrest instead of lowering the chair so much that the desk becomes too high.

## Step 2: check seat depth

You should have a small gap between the front edge of the seat and the back of your knees. Too much seat depth pushes you forward and makes the backrest useless. Too little depth leaves your thighs unsupported.

## Step 3: place lumbar support

The lumbar support belongs in the lower-back curve. It should support, not shove. I learned this the hard way with my first budget chair: the "lumbar" pad sat too high, so I kept sliding forward without noticing.

## Step 4: set armrests

Your elbows should rest lightly, shoulders down. If your armrests force the shoulders up, they are too high. If they keep you too far from the desk, remove them or lower them. Bad armrest height often shows up as neck tension, not elbow pain.

## Step 5: match the chair to the rest of the desk

The chair does not work alone. Once it is set, place the monitor at eye level and keep the keyboard close enough that you are not reaching. Use the [correct desk and chair height guide](/en/guides/correct-desk-and-chair-height/) to finish the setup.
