### Logitech Lift: the first one I would check for smaller hands

Logitech recommends the Lift for small to medium hands. Its published dimensions are **108 × 70 × 71 mm**, with a weight of **125 g**. Compared with the MX Vertical, length is a useful starting point: 108 rather than 120 mm. It cannot tell you exactly how the mouse will fit, but it helps if you already know your current mouse feels too large.

The Lift has a 57-degree angle, quiet clicks and a SmartWheel scroll wheel. It can pair with up to three devices and switch between them through Easy-Switch. The standard version includes a Logi Bolt receiver and also supports Bluetooth. On a laptop with few ports, leaving the USB connection free for another accessory can be useful.

I would choose it for the combination of size, quieter clicks and device switching. I would pass if USB-C charging is a requirement: it uses one AA battery. Logitech's battery-life estimate depends on usage; we have not measured its endurance during a particular work routine.

Lift Left is a separate product variant. Our [Logitech Lift profile](/en/catalog/mice/logitech-lift/) and the button below refer to the right-handed model. Swapping the primary buttons in your operating system does not turn that shell into the left-handed version. Check the exact item before ordering, particularly when an online store groups several colours and versions together.

### Logitech MX Vertical: a longer body with a rechargeable battery

The MX Vertical shares the Lift's 57-degree angle, but its body measures **120 × 79 × 78.5 mm**. Logitech's current specification lists **125 g**. Before choosing it because it belongs to the MX range, check that you can reach both the main and side buttons without sliding your hand forward.

Logitech documents three connection methods: Bluetooth, the Unifying receiver and USB-C cable. Easy-Switch supports up to three computers. The rechargeable battery avoids buying replacement cells. If using a cable as a connection when needed matters to you, that difference is more useful than selecting the highest DPI number.

Its sensor is adjustable from 400 to 4000 DPI. For documents and office applications, the goal is a setting that lets you select text and cross the screen with control, rather than always using the maximum. Options+ customization is available for Windows and macOS; basic pointer compatibility on another operating system does not imply support for that software.

I would consider it if the Lift body seems too short or if a rechargeable battery is preferable. The trade-offs are a right-handed design, a tall shape to fit in a bag, and Unifying rather than Bolt. We have not verified a quiet-click feature comparable to the Lift's. The [MX Vertical product profile](/en/catalog/mice/logitech-mx-vertical/) puts those differences alongside its specifications.

### ProtoArc EM11 NL: three connections, with limits worth reading

The checked EM11 NL offers two Bluetooth channels and a 2.4 GHz USB receiver. Its mode button switches between three devices. That is a useful arrangement for a work laptop, personal laptop and tablet, provided their operating systems fall within the stated compatibility.

The listing gives dimensions of **13.8 × 6.9 × 6.8 cm**, a 500 mAh battery and three sensitivity settings: **1000, 1600 and 2400 DPI**. The USB-C cable only charges it. If Bluetooth is off and the receiver is disconnected, plugging in that cable does not turn it into a USB data mouse.

There is a specific Mac limitation: the seller states that the forward and back buttons do not support macOS and that the buttons are not programmable. Basic pointer operation should not be confused with having the same shortcuts as a Logitech mouse configured through Options+.

I would consider it for switching between devices with a rechargeable battery. I would skip it if browser side buttons on a Mac are central to the way you work. We cannot claim that its wheel or materials feel better or worse than another model without testing or adequate comparative evidence. Check the stated feature set rather than reading an unverified quality judgement into the price.

The proposition is simple: connect the receiver and use the mouse without Bluetooth pairing. That also defines the limitation. A laptop with only USB-C ports may need an adapter for the receiver, so check the actual connector and available ports before ordering.

I would compare it if you prefer replaceable batteries and a receiver connection. I would not pick it for frequent switching between computers without moving the USB receiver. ## How to choose the best vertical ergonomic mouse for your desk

Check fit before comparing extras. Length and width provide a starting point, but button placement and thumb-rest shape matter too. With the mouse in your hand, you should be able to click without repeatedly repositioning your fingers or squeezing the shell to hold it still.

If you cannot try it yet, compare its dimensions with a mouse you know and look at views from above and from the side. A single isolated product photograph gives little sense of scale. A shorter mouse is not necessarily lighter: the Lift and MX Vertical illustrate why dimensions and weight need to be checked separately.

Next, review the conditions at your desk:

- Which hand you use, and the precise variant being sold.
- Available ports, and whether your employer allows Bluetooth or external receivers.
- Whether you need to switch between computers without pairing again.
- Which features need software and whether you can install it.
- How much room remains for mouse movement next to the keyboard.

Basic compatibility and customization are separate questions. A pointer working on Linux does not mean Options+ is available there. A hardware device-switching button is not the same feature as copying files between computers, either. File transfer can have additional software and network requirements.

Our [ergonomic keyboard comparison](/en/accessories/best-ergonomic-keyboards/) can help you think about the space left for the mouse. If a keyboard occupies most of the usable width, replacing the mouse without reviewing its location may leave your arm reaching just as far as before.

### Charging, data and receiver support need separate checks

Some mice charge through USB-C while continuing to send pointer data wirelessly. ProtoArc explicitly describes that behaviour for the EM11 NL. If a wired data connection is essential, look for it in the documentation; a photograph of a charging port is not sufficient evidence.

Receiver compatibility also needs a model-specific check. The Lift and MX Vertical are both Logitech products, but one uses Bolt and the other Unifying. If you want to share a receiver with a keyboard to free a USB port, check both products rather than assuming the brand name guarantees compatibility.

Replaceable batteries are not automatically a disadvantage. They let you resume use by changing cells, while an integrated rechargeable battery avoids that purchase. What matters is your preferred maintenance routine and the package contents. Here, the Anker excludes its AAA batteries and the Trust includes them. Neither arrangement tells us how the mouse will feel in your hand.

## What a vertical shape changes, and what we cannot promise

A vertical mouse changes hand orientation compared with a flat mouse. That does not establish that it prevents carpal tunnel syndrome, removes pain or reduces an individual's injury risk by a particular percentage. A manufacturer's promotional figures are not a guaranteed result at your desk.

[HSE's guidance on display screen equipment](https://www.hse.gov.uk/msd/dse/) recommends assessing the workstation as a whole and reducing risks through adjustments and changes of activity. It also notes that the causes of discomfort are not always obvious. This is workplace prevention guidance, not evidence that one of these mice treats a medical condition.

Before spending money, review desk and chair height, keyboard distance and arm support. The [home-office ergonomics guide](/en/guides/how-to-set-up-an-ergonomic-home-office/) is a starting point for that wider assessment. Persistent symptoms deserve professional advice; the position of a finger or an accessory advert is not enough to diagnose them.

### Trying a different grip without an arbitrary deadline

Begin with familiar tasks: selecting a paragraph, dragging a window and scrolling through a spreadsheet. Adjust pointer speed if necessary. Changing one variable at a time helps distinguish a grip issue from sensitivity or mouse placement.

There is no adaptation period we can guarantee. If buttons are hard to reach, you need to squeeze the shell, or discomfort develops, reassess the fit. Persisting for two weeks will not automatically make an unsuitable size a good purchase. Keep the packaging and check the return conditions for the specific offer before ordering.

A trackball addresses a different preference: moving the pointer with a ball without sliding the device across the desk. It may interest you because of space or how you prefer to control the cursor, but requires a different input action. We would not recommend it as a treatment based on a short description of discomfort either.
