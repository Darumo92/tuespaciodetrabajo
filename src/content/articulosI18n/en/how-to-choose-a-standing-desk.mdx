## Is a standing desk worth it?

Before the "how", the "why". A standing desk **does not cure back pain**: it is a tool that lowers the friction to move. The [Cornell University Ergonomics Web](https://ergo.human.cornell.edu/AHTutorials/ckpttoc.html) has argued for sit/stand alternation for over a decade. The key word is *alternate*: the desk helps only if you actually raise and lower it.

- **It is worth it if** you work from home several hours a day, struggle to sit still, or already carry lower-back or neck strain.
- **It is not worth it if** you use the desk for an odd hour a day or expect a magic fix that saves you from moving: no piece of furniture does that.

If you are unsure, I have an honest look at [the real pros and cons after two years of use](/en/guides/are-standing-desks-worth-it/) before you spend the money.

## Manual, electric or frame only: which type to choose

There are three formats, and it helps to be clear from the start:

- **Electric complete (desktop included).** Plug it in and it works. It is the simplest option and the one I recommend to most people. Entry-level examples include the [DEVAISE 120×60](/en/catalog/standing-desks/devaise-120x60/) or the [SONGMICS LSD134](/en/catalog/standing-desks/songmics-lsd134/).
- **Frame only (electric, no desktop).** You buy the frame and add your own top (or an IKEA one, or the one you already had). Ideal if you want a specific size or material, or an L-shaped setup. The [Duronic TM61](/en/catalog/standing-desks/duronic-tm61/) is a dual-motor frame example.
- **Manual (crank).** Cheaper and no electronics, but raising and lowering takes 30-40 seconds of cranking. That friction means almost everyone ends up leaving it fixed. I only recommend it if you will barely move it.

As a rule: if you do not have a clear reason to choose the desktop separately, go **electric complete**.

## The 5 criteria that really matter (in order)

This is where a good purchase is decided. Look at them in this order:

### 1. Height range
First, because if the desk does not reach your standing height, nothing else matters. A good range runs from about **62-72 cm (seated) to 118-125 cm (standing)**. The taller or shorter you are, the more it matters. Over 1.85 m, look for a top above 120 cm; under 1.65 m, one that drops below 68 cm.

### 2. Motor type: single or dual
- **Single motor:** cheaper, slower and wobbles with uneven loads (a heavy monitor on one side). Fine for light use.
- **Dual motor:** faster, quieter and handles unevenly distributed weight better. If you will switch several times a day or load monitors, it is the difference between using it and leaving it fixed.

### 3. Load capacity
Entry-level holds 70-80 kg; mid-range 100 kg; premium reaches 160 kg. A normal setup (monitor, laptop, keyboard, accessories) weighs 15-25 kg, so almost anything is plenty. But if you use a **clamp monitor arm** or a heavy ultrawide, aim for **100 kg minimum** for margin.

### 4. Stability at full height
The figure that appears least on spec sheets and shows up most in real use. At standing height, a cheap desk wobbles when you type or move the mouse, especially with two monitors. Stability depends on the frame (3-stage columns and wide feet help) and the dual motor. If you want a **stable** desk, this is the criterion to watch.

### 5. The desktop: real size and material
Measure your room before buying. For one monitor and a laptop, **120×60 cm** is the functional minimum; for external monitor + laptop beside it, **140×70 cm**; for two monitors or documents, **160×80 cm**. Leave at least 15 cm of margin on each side for cables and ventilation. On material, chipboard does the job; bamboo and solid wood (as in the [Oakywood Standing Desk Pro](/en/catalog/standing-desks/oakywood-standing-desk-pro/)) are a step up in finish and price.

**The extras** — memory presets, anti-collision, USB port or built-in sockets — are nice, but they come after the above. You will use the **presets** daily; **anti-collision** helps with kids or pets; **sockets/USB** are convenient but should not decide the purchase.

## How to calculate your correct height

A standing desk is useless if you leave it at a height that wrecks your shoulders. The quick way:

1. **Seated**, feet flat on the floor, elbows at 90°: measure floor to elbow. That is your seated height (for most, 68-76 cm).
2. **Standing**, elbows also at 90°: measure floor to elbow again (usually 100-118 cm by height).
3. Check the **desk's range covers both** and save them to the presets.

## The 20-8-2 rule: how long to stand

A very common question: how long should you stand? The most cited guideline is the **20-8-2 rule**: for every half hour, about **20 minutes sitting, 8 standing and 2 moving** (stand up, stretch, walk). It is not dogma (the exact number does not matter): the idea is not spending hours in the same posture. A standing desk with presets makes sticking to it as easy as pressing a button, which is why it works.

## How much to spend: budget, mid or premium

- **Budget (under €150):** single-motor electric complete desks, 70-80 kg, to start without risk. Examples: [DEVAISE 120×60](/en/catalog/standing-desks/devaise-120x60/), [SONGMICS LSD134](/en/catalog/standing-desks/songmics-lsd134/).
- **Mid (€150-400):** dual motor, 100-160 kg, presets and better stability. The sweet spot for daily remote work. Examples: [FlexiSpot E7](/en/catalog/standing-desks/flexispot-e7/), [Maidesite T2 Pro Plus](/en/catalog/standing-desks/maidesite-t2-pro-plus/), [Duronic TM61](/en/catalog/standing-desks/duronic-tm61/).
- **Premium (over €500):** bamboo or solid wood, design finish, long warranties. Examples: [FlexiSpot Q8](/en/catalog/standing-desks/flexispot-q8/), [Oakywood Standing Desk Pro](/en/catalog/standing-desks/oakywood-standing-desk-pro/), [Eleva Pro](/en/catalog/standing-desks/eleva-pro/).

The big quality jump is from budget to mid; from mid to premium you mostly pay for materials and design.
