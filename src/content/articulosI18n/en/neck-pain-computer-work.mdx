Neck pain from computer work usually starts quietly: a little stiffness after lunch, then tight shoulders, then the habit of rubbing the back of your neck during calls. In many home offices the cause is obvious once you look at the setup: the screen is too low and the body has adapted around it.

## The main causes

The laptop-on-desk position is the classic problem. Your eyes look down, the head moves forward and the neck works harder than it should. A monitor that sits too low creates the same issue. A mouse too far away can also pull one shoulder forward all day.

## Fix the screen first

Bring the screen up until the top area is near eye level. Use a monitor, monitor arm, riser or laptop stand. If you lift a laptop, add an external keyboard and mouse. Do not solve one ergonomic problem by creating another.

## Then check the chair and arms

If your armrests are too high, the shoulders stay tense. If they are too low, the arms hang and pull on the neck. Your elbows should rest naturally close to your body.

## Breaks matter

Even a good setup fails if you freeze for three hours. Use short movement breaks: neck rotations, shoulder rolls, standing for calls, or a quick walk. If you also have wrist symptoms, read the [carpal tunnel prevention guide](/en/guides/carpal-tunnel-remote-work-prevention/).
