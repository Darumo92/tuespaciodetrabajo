Desk stretches work when they are short enough to actually happen. You do not need a yoga mat between meetings; you need small resets that stop your neck, hips and wrists from staying in the same position all day.

## A two-minute chair reset

Start with five slow shoulder rolls. Then turn your head gently left and right, keeping the shoulders down. Open the chest by clasping your hands behind your back or placing your hands on the chair sides. Finish by standing up, extending the hips and taking ten slow steps.

## Wrist and forearm reset

Open and close your hands ten times. Then stretch one arm forward with the palm down and gently pull the fingers toward you. Repeat with the palm up. Keep it mild; the goal is blood flow, not forcing range.

## Hip reset

Sit tall, place one ankle over the opposite knee and lean slightly forward. You should feel the stretch in the outer hip, not the knee. Change sides after 20-30 seconds.

Combine these breaks with a better setup. If your pain keeps coming back, see [back pain when working from home](/en/guides/back-pain-working-from-home/) and [neck pain from computer work](/en/guides/neck-pain-computer-work/).
